# ReMoRehab

Guided mirror therapy for stroke recovery, in a browser — plus an open 3D-printable headset that
turns a phone you already own into the display.

**Congressional App Challenge entry, Maryland's 1st congressional district.**

Site: https://www.aranyoing.com/remorehab/ · [Open the app](https://www.aranyoing.com/remorehab/app/)

---

## Why

Salisbury sits in Wicomico County on Maryland's Lower Eastern Shore, where the need for rehab is
high and the supply of it is thin at the same time. CDC PLACES puts stroke prevalence at **3.4% of
adults in Wicomico and 4.0% in neighbouring Somerset** — the highest in Maryland — against a state
county median of 2.9%. Meanwhile nearly the entire Eastern Shore carries federal designation as a
medically underserved area and a health professional shortage area, and the five Maryland counties
with the fewest primary care physicians per capita are all on the Shore.

Nationally, more than **795,000 people have a stroke each year**, stroke is a leading cause of
serious long-term disability, and more than half of survivors aged 65 and over are left with reduced
mobility. The therapy that limits that disability is mostly repetition — done often, early, and kept
up. The barrier is rarely that the exercise is hard. It is getting to a room with a therapist in it
three times a week for three months.

Every figure is sourced, with data years and limitations, on the
[numbers page](https://www.aranyoing.com/remorehab/data/).

## What this is

**The app** (`app/`) is a single web page. It opens the camera, mirrors the working limb across the
centre line so the brain sees two good limbs moving together, paces timed sets with an audible cue,
counts reps and keeps a session log. It has a stereo mode for the printed headset. The video is
drawn to a canvas and never leaves the device; the log is `localStorage` only. No build step, no
dependencies, no backend.

**The headset** (`stl/`, `headset-source/`) is an eleven-part printable shroud: faceted shell,
adjustable 56–68 mm lens spacing, a sculpted face rim, a hex cooling grille and a central light
barrier. It is generated by a parametric Python script rather than hand-modelled, so every dimension
is a constant you can change and regenerate.

## Running it

The site is static — open `index.html`, or serve the folder:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Camera access needs HTTPS or `localhost`, so use the served URL rather than a `file://` path.

Regenerating the headset:

```bash
python3 -m venv venv && ./venv/bin/pip install numpy trimesh manifold3d mapbox-earcut rtree
./venv/bin/python headset-source/build.py     # writes stl/
./venv/bin/python headset-source/verify.py    # interference, clearance, printability
```

`verify.py` checks that no two assembled parts overlap, that the lens pods clear the carrier and
septum across the full spacing range, that the carrier fits through the shell's taper to its seat,
and it measures unsupported overhang per part by ray-casting every downward-facing face.

## Honest limits

This is a student project, not a medical device. It has not been through a clinical trial and is not
FDA cleared. Mirror therapy itself has moderate-quality evidence behind it — the 2018 Cochrane review
found a standardised mean difference of 0.47 (95% CI 0.27 to 0.67) on upper-limb motor function at
the end of treatment across 36 trials — but durability is genuinely unproven, with only two trials
and 88 participants measuring anything at six months. Nothing here replaces a therapist. It is a way
to get more repetitions in on the days when reaching a clinic is not possible.
